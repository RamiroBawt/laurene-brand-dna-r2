B · Intimate — The Intimate Recommender

The person you'd trust to pick a restaurant.

The register

Baseline. Warm, candlelit, italic-color-accent serif headlines in brass-warm, Pinyon Script signatures across multiple touchpoints, photography full-bleed with warm gradient. Personal trust as the authority signal.

What it uses

Full-bleed candlelit photography · italic-color-accent in brass-warm · Pinyon Script as repeated warmth marker · Playfair Display 60–110pt headlines

What it rules out

Sparse magazine layouts · ultra-tracked caps · stark cream paper · cool restraint · single-flourish ornamental use

The feel

Like a contact's text recommendation. Warm, immediate, personal.

B · Luxe — Intimate Luxe

Your contact at the boutique hotel.

The register

Refined evolution. Same intimacy, more breathing room. Champagne-gold replaces brass-warm. Generous margins, deliberate negative space. Nº-prefixed numerals as a small editorial signature. Thin gold hairline rules. Pinyon Script used sparingly — one signature per piece.

What it uses

Champagne-gold accent · Nº numerals · generous margins · thin gold hairline rules · refined photography · single Pinyon signature

What it rules out

Full-bleed crowding · multiple Pinyon flourishes per piece · informal warmth without composure

The feel

Like the concierge note slipped under your suite door. Warm, but considered.

B · Vogue — Editorial Luxe

The cover, the spread, the signature.

The register

Vogue-magazine register. Cream paper dominates frames. Massive Didone-scale display headlines (110–170pt). Ultra-tracked caps (0.36–0.42em) for eyebrows. Italic + roman pairings in headlines (Wine & Food). Masthead-style top rule with Nº, issue, volume. Single Pinyon flourish per piece — ornament, not warmth-marker. Asymmetric compositions, dramatic photography placement.

What it uses

Didone-scale display type · masthead top rule (label · Nº · vol) · matte editorial gold · ultra-tracked caps · italic + roman headline pairings · single ornamental Pinyon

What it rules out

Casual warmth · multiple sigs · brass-warm orange · full-bleed-only compositions · sans-serif anything

The feel

Like opening a Vogue Living spread. The brand IS the publication.

The right tier is the one that doesn't make you flinch. Baseline reads as warm and present — like a text recommendation. Luxe reads as composed — like the boutique-hotel note. Vogue reads as editorial — the cover, the spread, the signature.

You can also blend: lock the Vogue masthead and numerals for some content types (market reports, neighborhood guides), keep Luxe for restaurant picks and field notes, keep baseline for the personal pinned intro. The system holds together as long as one register dominates the feed visually.
